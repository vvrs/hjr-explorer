Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability, Engineered & Learned

Collision checks ask where your drone is right now. Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) asks the only question physics actually cares about: from which states is a crash already inevitable—even if your motors command maximum tilt-braking this instant while the worst admissible wind gust pushes your tail?

Chapter 02 First Principles & Geometry

Lifting Shapes into Surfaces: From 1 Line of DP to the HJI Variational Inequality

Tracking the boundary of an unsafe set directly is a geometric nightmare: boundaries stretch, collide, pinch off, and develop sharp corners. Osher, Sethian, Mitchell, Bayen, and Tomlin solved this with one profound trick: represent every set as the waterline of a higher-dimensional surface.

Step 1 · Encode Failure as a Signed Margin Surface \(l(x)\)

Pick a continuous function \(l : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}\) whose sign tells you whether state \(x\) is safe right now: \[ \mathcal{F} = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n : l(x) < 0 \} \quad \text{(failure set)}. \] For a wall at \(p_{\text{wall}}=10\text{ m}\), \(l(p,v) = 10 - p\) is literally distance-to-wall in meters.

Step 2 · Play a Zero-Sum Differential Game Over Horizon \(\tau\)

Along a trajectory \(\xi_{x}^{u,d}(s)\) starting at \(x\) under control \(u(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}\) and disturbance \(d(\cdot) \in \mathcal{D}\), the closest approach to failure over \([0, \tau]\) is \(\min_{s\in[0,\tau]} l(\xi_x^{u,d}(s))\). Control maximizes this worst-case clearance; wind/intruder minimizes it:

The HJR Value Function (Worst-Case Minimum Margin) \[ V(x, \tau) \;=\; \max_{u(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}} \; \min_{d(\cdot) \in \mathcal{D}} \; \min_{s \in [0, \tau]} \; l\!\left(\xi_x^{u,d}(s)\right) \]
\(\min_s l\): closest approach on \([0,\tau]\) \(\min_d\): adversarial wind/intruder \(\max_u\): optimal evasive control \(V(x,\tau) < 0 \iff x \in \text{BRT}(\tau)\)

Step 3 · Dynamic Programming + Taylor Limit = HJI PDE

Split \([0, \tau + \delta]\) into "right now" (\(s=0\)) versus "after a tiny step \(\delta\)": \[ V(x, \tau + \delta) = \min\!\Big\{\, l(x),\; \max_{u\in\mathcal{U}} \min_{d\in\mathcal{D}} V\big(x + \delta f(x,u,d),\, \tau\big) \Big\}. \] Substitute \(V(x + \delta f, \tau) \approx V(x,\tau) + \delta\,\nabla_x V \cdot f(x,u,d)\), divide by \(\delta \to 0\), and you obtain the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs Variational Inequality (HJI-VI):

Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs Variational Inequality \[ \min\!\Big\{\, l(x) - V(x,\tau),\;\; H\big(x, \nabla_x V(x,\tau)\big) - \frac{\partial V}{\partial \tau} \,\Big\} = 0, \qquad V(x,0) = l(x) \] \[ \text{where}\quad H(x, \lambda) = \max_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \min_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \; \lambda^\top f(x, u, d). \]
Set (BRS) vs. Tube (BRT) — Why the \(\min\{l - V, \dots\}\) Ratchet is Crucial

Without the \(l(x) - V\) floor, solving \(\partial_\tau V = H\) computes the Backward Reachable Set (BRS): states forced to be inside the obstacle at exact instant \(\tau\). If a fast drone punches straight through a thin pillar at \(t = 0.6\text{ s}\) and exits into open air at \(t = 2.0\text{ s}\), the BRS at \(\tau=2.0\text{ s}\) calls that state safe! The variational inequality acts as a one-way ratchet (\(\partial_\tau V \le 0\), \(V \le l\)) that remembers every mid-flight collision—producing the Backward Reachable Tube (BRT). Chapter 03 Dynamics & Non-Triviality

If You Can Stop Instantly, the Reachable Tube Is Just the Obstacle

Before training a neural network or allocating megabytes of RAM for HJR, ask one diagnostic question: what physical dynamics stop the vehicle from halting instantaneously?

Model A · Velocity-Controlled Point (\(\dot{\mathbf{p}} = \mathbf{u}\))

BRT = Obstacle (0 m expansion)

Commanding velocity directly (\(|u_i| \le 1\), no wind)—the exact model used in NeuroHJR—gives \(H(x, \nabla V) = \|\nabla V\|_1 \ge 0\). Plugging \(V(x,\tau) \equiv l(x)\) satisfies \(\min\{l - V, H - \partial_\tau V\} = \min\{0, \|\nabla l\|_1\} = 0\) identically! Safety reachability adds zero information beyond a raw distance check.

Model B · Multirotor Double Integrator (\(\ddot{\mathbf{p}} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{d}\))

Directional Braking Shadows (4D–6D)

Horizontal acceleration is bounded by max pitch/roll tilt \(a_{\max} = g\tan\theta_{\max}\). Obstacles cast velocity-dependent "braking shadows" upwind of your motion vector; swerving and braking blend automatically along \(\mathbf{a}^* = a_{\max} \nabla_{\mathbf{v}} V / \|\nabla_{\mathbf{v}} V\|\).

Model C · Fixed-Wing / Cruise VTOL (3D Dubins Air3D)

Twisted 3D Turn-Radius Tubes (Cannot Stop!)

A fixed-wing drone or winged delivery VTOL stalls below \(v_{\min}\): \(\dot{x} = v\cos\psi + d_x,\; \dot{y} = v\sin\psi + d_y,\; \dot{\psi} = \omega\). Because it cannot stop, any state closer than its turn envelope at heading \(\psi\) is doomed—carving a helical 3D horn in \((x, y, \psi)\)!

Chapter 04 Planning & Control of Drones

Three Ways HJR Connects to a Real Drone Autonomy Stack

HJR is not just "an obstacle avoidance controller." In modern aerial robotics, it plugs into planning and control in three distinct ways: (A) 4D Multi-Obstacle Safety Envelopes, (B) Reach-Avoid Landing / Go-Around Funnels, and (C) FaSTrack Planner-Tracker Error Bounds.

1. Avoid vs. Reach-Avoid (Precision Dock Landing)

Landing a delivery or inspection drone on a \(1.8\text{ m}\) charging dock in gusty crosswinds is a Reach-Avoid game (Margellos & Lygeros 2011; Fisac et al. 2015): reach the safe touchdown box \(\mathcal{T} = \{r(x) \ge 0\}\) (centered on pad, gentle sink rate \(v_z \in [-1.4, 0.3]\text{ m/s}\)) while avoiding deck strikes, coaming walls, and hard slams \(\mathcal{F} = \{l(x) < 0\}\):

Reach-Avoid Dynamic Programming Step \[ V_{\text{RA}}(x, \tau+\delta) = \min\!\Big\{\, l(x),\; \max\!\Big\{\, r(x),\; \max_u \min_d V_{\text{RA}}\big(x + \delta f,\, \tau\big) \Big\} \Big\} \]

Whenever a wind gust pushes the descending drone across \(V_{\text{RA}}(x) = 0\), safe touchdown is no longer guaranteed—triggering an automatic go-around climb before the aircraft gets trapped near the deck!

2. FaSTrack: Decoupling Fast Planners from Nonlinear Quadrotors

Global planners (A*, RRT*, Jump Point Search) run in milliseconds on 3D voxel maps only because they pretend the drone is a kinematic point \(\dot{\hat{p}} = \hat{v}\). When a real 6D/10D quadrotor (\(\dot{p}=v,\, \dot{v}=g\tan\theta+d,\, \dot{\theta}=\omega\)) tries to track sharp corners in wind, pitch lag causes corner overshoot into obstacles.

FaSTrack (Herbert, Chen, Bansal & Tomlin, CDC 2017) treats the planner itself as a bounded adversary in relative error coordinates \(e = p - \hat{p}\)! Offline HJR computes the tightest Tracking Error Bound (TEB) bubble and optimal pitch-rate tracker \(\omega^*(e, v, \theta)\). Online, you simply inflate map obstacles by TEB and let any fast geometric planner run freely—map-independent, zero online PDE solves!

Chapter 06 Physics-Informed Neural Networks (DeepReach)

Breaking the \(N^d\) Grid Wall—and the Three Traps That Break Naive PINNs

At 50 points per axis, a 4D value array takes 25 MB, a 6D quadrotor takes 62 GB, and a 10D quadrotor-with-payload takes 390 Petabytes. A Physics-Informed Neural Network replaces the grid with smooth weights \(V_\theta(x, \tau)\), computing exact \(\nabla_x V_\theta, \partial_\tau V_\theta\) via automatic differentiation at random collocation points.

Why DeepReach (Bansal & Tomlin, ICRA 2021) Works

Training a PINN on a first-order Hamilton-Jacobi variational inequality is notoriously unforgiving because \(V\) is non-smooth (viscosity kinks) and information propagates causally outward from \(\tau = 0\). Three architectural ingredients separate a working HJR PINN from a broken one:

  1. Solve the Time-Dependent PDE \(V_\theta(x, \tau)\), Never Free Steady-State: The steady inequality \(\min\{l(x) - V, H(x,\nabla V)\} = 0\) admits infinitely many spurious solutions—any constant \(V(x) \equiv c \le \min l(x)\) gives \(l - c \ge 0\) and \(H(x, \mathbf{0}) = 0\), yielding zero PDE loss while marking the entire universe unsafe!
  2. Exact Boundary Parametrization at \(\tau = 0\): Write \(V_\theta(x, \tau) = l(x) + \frac{\tau}{\tau_{\max}} \text{NN}_\theta(x, \tau)\) so \(V_\theta(x, 0) \equiv l(x)\) holds identically with zero boundary error.
  3. Sinusoidal Activations (SIREN) + Temporal Curriculum: Grow sampled \(\tau \in [0, \tau_{\text{cur}}(k)]\) linearly from \(0 \to \tau_{\max}\) so characteristics propagate outward from \(\tau=0\), and use \(\sin(\omega_0 z)\) activations whose derivatives \(\cos(\omega_0 z)\) represent sharp gradient transitions far better than ReLU (whose second derivative is zero almost everywhere!).
Self-Supervised DeepReach HJI-VI Residual Loss \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{HJI}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \Big( \min\!\big\{\, l(x_i) - V_\theta(x_i, \tau_i),\;\; H(x_i, \nabla_x V_\theta) - \partial_\tau V_\theta \,\big\} \Big)^2 \]
Zero ground-truth grid labels needed 2nd-order backprop through \(\nabla_x V_\theta\)

Chapter 07 Forensic Paper Dissection · arXiv:2512.01897

Reading NeuroHJR Critically: Six Mathematical Flaws & How to Repair Them

Halder, Majumder, Rakshith, Shah & Sundaram (NeuroHJR, Dec 2025) propose a \(3\times 128\) \(\tanh\) network \((P_x, P_y) \mapsto (u_x, u_y, V)\) for a 2D velocity-controlled UGV (\(\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{u},\; |u_i| \le 1\)) crossing a \(45\text{ m} \times 45\text{ m}\) field with 10 obstacles (\(R=2\text{ m},\; \delta=1\text{ m} \implies R_d=3\text{ m}\)). With the first-principles tools from Chapters 2–6, we can audit every equation directly.

Flaw 1 · Eq. (14) Dynamics

Trivial Reachable Set (\(\mathcal{B} \equiv \mathcal{F}\))

Because \(\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{u}\) has zero inertia and zero disturbance, the vehicle stops in \(0.0\text{ m}\). True safety HJR expands the \(3\text{ m}\) obstacle discs by 0.00 m. What the vehicle actually needs is shortest-path routing (static Eikonal HJB), not safety reachability.

Flaw 2 · Eqs. (18) & (21) PDE

Missing Stage Cost \(\implies \nabla V \equiv \mathbf{0}\)

Minimum-time HJB requires stage cost \(+1\): \(1 + \min_{\mathbf{u}} \nabla V\cdot\mathbf{u} = 1 - \|\nabla V\|_1 = 0\). Dropping \(+1\) in Eq. (18) yields \(-\|\nabla V\|_1 = 0 \implies V = \text{const}\)! Worse, Eq. (21) minimizes \((\nabla V_\theta \cdot \mathbf{u}_\theta)^2\), satisfied trivially when \(\nabla V_\theta = \mathbf{0}\) or \(\mathbf{u}_\theta \perp \nabla V_\theta\).

Flaw 3 · Eq. (23) Obstacle Loss

Identically Zero Parameter Gradient!

Eq. (23) writes \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{obstacle}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum (\max(0, R_d - d_i))^2\) where \(d_i = \|\mathbf{P}^{(i)} - \mathbf{p}_o\|\) depends only on fixed collocation coordinates \(\mathbf{P}^{(i)}\), not on network outputs \((u_\theta, V_\theta)\)! Thus \(\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{obstacle}} \equiv \mathbf{0}\) (and on safe-region points \(d_i > R_d\), even the value is zero!).

Flaw 4 · Eqs. (5), (8), (9) Value

Unsigned Distances & Missing Obstacles

\(V_F(x,0) = \min_{y\in\mathcal{S}_0}\|x-y\| \ge 0\) and \(V_B(x,T) = \min_{y\in\mathcal{T}}\|x-y\| \ge 0\) never reference obstacles at all, and \(V = \max\{V_F, -V_B\} \ge V_F \ge 0\) everywhere—making the paper's safety condition "\(V(x,t) \ge 0\)" tautologically true at every point, including inside obstacles!

Flaw 5 · Map Overfitting vs. Speed

10,000 Epochs per Map vs. 5 ms Dijkstra

Obstacles are hardcoded into the loss rather than passed to the network: moving one cylinder requires retraining from scratch. Meanwhile, solving the exact 2D Eikonal HJB equation on a \(181\times 181\) grid takes ~5 milliseconds right in your browser below!

How to Repair It Properly

From NeuroHJR to Verified DeepReach

(1) Model real 4D–10D inertia/turn/gust dynamics; (2) enforce time-dependent HJI-VI (or \(1 - \|\nabla T\| = 0\) for time-to-goal); (3) condition \(V_\theta(x, \tau; o_1,\dots,o_k)\) on relative obstacle features or local ESDF; (4) certify \(\{V_\theta > \delta\}\) via conformal rollout bounds.

Chapter 09 Industrial Feasibility & Spotting Use Cases

Is HJR Actually Feasible in Production Drone Stacks? The Honest Engineering Verdict

Yes—when applied to the right 3D–5D relative subproblem, and almost never as a monolithic full-map neural planner. Here is how Staff Autonomy Engineers at companies building Skydio/Zipline/Wing/Defense-class UAVs spot high-ROI HJR use cases and ship them safely.

Tier 1 · Ships Today on MCU / CPU (High ROI)

1D–4D Offline Lookup Tables + CBVF/RTA

Where to spot it: Dock landing go-around funnels (4D), fixed-wing/VTOL Air3D intruder deconfliction (3D, like ACAS sXu), geofence braking envelopes in wind (2D–4D), and battery-vs-headwind return-to-home alarms (3D).
Footprint: \(0.1\text{–}4.5\text{ MB}\) quantized LUT, \(< 3\,\mu\text{s}\) trilinear/quadrilinear lookup in C++ on STM32H7 or Jetson.

Tier 1 · Ships Today with Any Planner

FaSTrack Tracking-Error Bubbles

Where to spot it: Fast A*/RRT*/MPPI planners in dense forests, warehouses, or urban canyons where agile quadrotors cut corners under gusts.
Why it wins: Solved once per airframe in relative coordinates (independent of obstacle map!). Online cost is literally inflating your ESDF/voxel radius by \(\text{TEB}\) meters + a \(1\,\mu\text{s}\) table lookup.

Tier 2 · Emerging Production (Orin / Thor GPU)

ASTM F3269 Run-Time Assurance & Verified 6D–10D PINNs

Where to spot it: Wrapping unverified end-to-end RL / Vision-Language-Action flight policies (RTA Simplex switch), or 8D–12D coupled dynamics that cannot decompose (slung-load cable swing, aggressive SE(3) perching, variable-payload envelopes).
Rule: Never fly an unverified PINN; always pair DeepReach + Conformal \(\delta\)-shift + verified backup controller.

▸ 4-Phase Production Rollout Checklist (How to Ship HJR Without Grounding the Fleet)

Phase 1 — Flight-Log Disturbance Identification: Never guess \(d_{\max}\). Extract position/velocity tracking residuals from 100+ hours of windy flight logs by altitude band to set empirical 99.9th-percentile gust and latency bounds.

Phase 2 — Offline Validation Against Closed Forms: Validate your 3D/4D grid or PINN on 1D head-on and oblique slices against analytical braking parabolas; add the measured discretization optimism \(\varepsilon_{\text{grid}}\) directly into your switching/barrier level.

Phase 3 — Shadow-Mode Telemetry (Zero Actuation Authority): Deploy the lookup table / PINN onboard in passive shadow mode alongside PX4/ArduPilot. Log every \(V(x) < \varepsilon\) alarm during aggressive human/autonomy flights to measure false-positive conservatism before closing the loop.

Phase 4 — Closed-Loop CBVF-QP / ASTM F3269 RTA Switch: Enable smooth CBVF filtering (for nominal comfort) backed by a hard \(V(x) \le \varepsilon_{\text{crit}}\) RTA emergency maneuver (brake-to-hover or go-around climb).
